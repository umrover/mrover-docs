---
title: "Global A*"
---

# Context
One of the target AR tags in the mission will be positioned on top of a hill. Right now the rover only knows about its environment through what its cameras can see, so it drives a straight line toward the GNSS waypoint and relies on local A* to avoid whatever shows up in front of it. On hilly terrain this can send the rover into dead ends or unnavigable slopes.

# Problem
The rover lacks information about its environment outside of its cameras. We need a way to plan a long-term route that accounts for the terrain before the rover ever sees it, while still reacting to obstacles that the terrain data does not capture.

# Solution
Use a static costmap generated from DEM (Digital Elevation Model) data of the mission site in Utah to generate a longer, global A* trajectory.

- The path generated by global A* replaces the straight line trajectory we currently use.
- This trajectory is used as a baseline for the rover to follow.
- Local A* still runs to make sure the rover doesn't run into objects unknown to the static costmap (e.g. a rock that doesn't show up in the DEM).
- The cost used by local A* is the **max** of the dynamic (ZED) costmap and the static costmap.

## Architecture



Initially, we will store ~0.25 miles squared of costmap data. The mission site is around a square mile, so this may need to grow or be swapped out as the rover travels.

## Additional Considerations
- Regenerate the global trajectory when the rover travels a significant distance from the given path.
- Use drone waypoints to either reduce the cost in the given areas or chain global A* paths together.
- Rover testing is needed to determine these parameters (how far is "significant", how much to reduce cost, etc.) and how effective they are.
- Increase environmental variability within the simulator so it better illustrates situations similar to the mission.
